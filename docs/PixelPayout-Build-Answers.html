02 The fraud problem, layered

Your question: "everyone will just create new accounts and get UC after UC." Correct instinct, and the answer is six cheap layers rather than one expensive system. Roughly four days of work total.

Layer 0

Make the prize not worth a human's time

This is the strongest defence and it is free — you already have it. First pack ≈ $0.90, requiring ~30 days × 20 minutes of real play. That is roughly 10 hours of human labour for 90 cents, about $0.09/hour. Manual farming is already uneconomic in every country on earth.

Which means only automation actually threatens you, and that narrows the whole problem. Everything below exists to stop machines, not people.

Layer 1

Player-ID linking — already built, and your best asset

playerLinks binds one game account to one app account. A farmer with 50 app accounts needs 50 PUBG accounts to receive into, and fifty accounts holding 60 UC each are far less sellable than one holding 3,000.

This is why refusing cash is an anti-fraud feature, not just a policy decision. Cash is fungible and farmable; 60 UC scattered across 50 throwaway game accounts is close to worthless. Say this out loud in your marketing — it is both true and reassuring.

One addition: require the linked player ID to be unchanged for 14 days before a first redeem. Blocks the create-throwaway-redeem-sell cycle at almost no cost to honest users, who set their ID once and never touch it.

Layer 2

Play Integrity on the redemption call only

Kills emulator farms, which are the only economically viable way to attack you. Full detail in section 03.

Layer 3

Time gates, not just action gates

Minimum 21 days account age and activity on ≥15 distinct days before the first redeem. A farmer can parallelise accounts but cannot parallelise the calendar — 100 accounts still means waiting three weeks, during which your velocity rules can see the cluster form.

Store it as a count of distinct UTC days with activity. You already stamp FIELD_LAST_RESET_TIME per day; this is a counter alongside it, not new infrastructure.

Layer 4

Cluster review at approval time

You approve manually anyway. Before approving a first redeem, check four things: same IP prefix, same device model plus install within the same hour, referral graph shape (one referrer whose twenty referees all redeem on day 21), and identical play timing patterns.

This is an admin query, not a machine-learning system. Do not buy a fraud vendor and do not build a model. At your scale a sorted list is better than either.

Layer 5

Hold the first payout 24–48 hours

Kills instant-gratification farming and costs honest users nothing if you tell them: "checked by hand, sent within 48 hours." Manual review sounds like a weakness; framed as a person verifying every payout, it is a trust feature. It also buys the window in which a cluster becomes visible.

What you will get wrong if you're not careful

Never hard-block on any single signal. Play Integrity fails legitimately on old devices, devices without Play Services, some Chinese ROMs and custom Android builds. Users in exactly your target markets. Route failures to manual review, never to an outright refusal — a false rejection on a real user's first payout is the most expensive mistake in this whole document, because that user tells everyone.

03 Play Integrity, in full

You asked what it is, how to implement it, the benefits, the loopholes and the policy requirements. All five.

What it is

Google Play's anti-abuse attestation. Your server asks "is this genuinely my unmodified app, from Play, on a real Android device?" and receives a verdict cryptographically signed by Google. Three verdicts matter:

VerdictWhat it tells you
appRecognitionVerdictPLAY_RECOGNIZED means the APK is yours, unmodified, from Play. This is the one that kills your repackaged-APK problem — the client lying about adWatched that your own comments already flag.
deviceRecognitionVerdictMEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY means a genuine Android device, not an emulator. Require this for payouts. MEETS_BASIC_INTEGRITY is weaker and passes on some modified devices — do not accept it alone.
appLicensingVerdictLICENSED means this Google account obtained the app from Play. A soft account-uniqueness signal, useful as a review input.

How to implement

  1. Add the dependency: com.google.android.play:integrity.
  2. Server generates a random nonce and returns it to the client. This is what stops token replay — skip it and the whole thing is decorative.
  3. Client requests a token via IntegrityManager.requestIntegrityToken(...) with that nonce and your cloud project number.
  4. Client sends the token to your Cloud Function.
  5. Server decodes it through the Play Integrity API and checks: the nonce matches the one it issued, requestPackageName is yours, the timestamp is fresh (a minute or two), and the verdicts are acceptable.
The quota rule that decides your architecture

Standard requests are free up to about 10,000 per day. That sounds generous until you call it per quiz — 1,000 DAU × 26 activities is 26,000 calls and you are over on day one. Call it on redemption and referral payout only. At 100 redeems a day you use 1% of the free tier, and those are the only two events where money actually moves.

Loopholes — be honest about these

  • It does not stop a real person with five cheap real phones. Nothing does, cheaply. Layer 0 is what makes that unprofitable.
  • It gives you no stable device identifier. Deliberately, for privacy. It is not a replacement for ANDROID_ID; it is a different question answered well.
  • It produces false negatives on real users — see the warning above.
  • Without nonce checking it is trivially replayable. Do not skip step 2.

Policy and cost

No special Play approval is required; it is a first-party API and it is free. You must declare the device signals it processes in your Play Data Safety form. Implementation including server verification: two to three days.

04 Funnel answers

"First launch is standard — how can we do it differently without changing the whole app?"

You don't redesign, you reorder. Three changes, none architectural:

  1. Guest sign-in — removes the wall entirely (below).
  2. Onboarding slide 3 — replace the generic "Cash Out" promise with a real payout screenshot, a redacted receipt, and "X players paid this week". Same slide, different content.
  3. Land on Play, not Home. First run should have them in a game inside 30 seconds. Home is a dashboard, and a dashboard means nothing before you have anything on it.

"How difficult is guest sign-in in our current app?"

Two to three days, and it is the highest-ROI funnel change available to you. Your instinct about Google accounts is exactly right — people will not hand a random app their Google account, and guest sign-in is the direct answer to that objection.

Firebase Anonymous Auth gives a real UID immediately; linkWithCredential later upgrades the same UID, so nothing is lost. Concretely in your app:

  • Enable Anonymous in the Firebase console.
  • OnboardingActivity signs in anonymously instead of routing to Auth.
  • buildNewUserProfile must run for anonymous users too — you already have economy/signup.ts for this.
  • Gate redemption, referral submission and leaderboard entry on !user.isAnonymous.
  • Prompt the upgrade at the moment they try to redeem — peak trust, peak motivation.
  • Move the androidId duplicate check from account creation to the link step.

The one genuinely fiddly case: an anonymous user links a Google account that already exists. Standard resolution is to warn, then sign into the existing account and discard the anonymous progress. Decide it deliberately or it will surprise you in production.

"First earn gives XP not stars — how would you fix that without changing the whole app?"

Nothing architectural. Level 2 is 50 XP — five correct quizzes or one decent game run, i.e. minutes away — and it already pays 5★. The design is fine; nobody told the user about it.

One dialog after the first completed activity:

+10 XP. XP levels you up — and every level pays ★ Stars. Level 2 is 40 XP away and pays 5★.

Then make the level-2 moment loud; you already have LevelUpNotifier. One string, one dialog, no refactor.

"Currency confusion — what sentence?"

Better as a strip than a sentence. One row on Home, tiny:

Play  →  XP  →  Level  →  ★ Stars  →  UC

And the string, used in the redeem sheet and the level-rewards note:

Games and quizzes pay XP. Levels, streaks and goals pay ★. Stars buy UC.

Three clauses, three currencies, no ambiguity about which one the loop actually gives you.

"Payout feed at the top — Home is already congested."

Fair, and you're right that a full card is too much. Don't add a card — add a single-line ticker under the points header, roughly 40dp, rotating every four seconds: "Joh****23 received 60 UC · 8 min ago". You already have payoutFeedRow and sheet_payout_feed.xml; this is a move plus a marquee, not new code.

Second free win: put the lifetime paid counter inside the existing stars card as a subtitle. Zero extra vertical space, and it is the single most persuasive number you will ever display.

"The countdown needs daily XP tracking — can we just say 100 diamonds in 90 days at 20 min/day?"

Yes, and it's the better trade. A personalised countdown needs rate tracking and a projection model, and it will be wrong for most users anyway. A static, conservative, honest claim costs one string and does more work:

  • Put it in the Play listing, where it filters out badly-matched users before they install and one-star you.
  • Put it per pack on the redeem sheet: "about 30 days at 20 minutes a day".
  • Under-promise deliberately. A user who arrives in 26 days when you said 30 becomes an advocate. One who arrives in 34 when you said 30 writes a review.

"Cold start — is minInstances worth the cost?"

Do the free thing first. A fire-and-forget warm ping at app open, with a warm: true early return before any side effects, covers most of it at roughly 6% of the free invocation tier. Only if that proves insufficient, set minInstances: 1 on exactly two functions — startGameSession and claimReward — and never broadly, given the CPU quota failure your own setGlobalOptions comment already records.

"Reset the interstitial counter when a rewarded ad loads rather than suppressing after it?"

Functionally close to what noteRewardedShown already does, and either is defensible. Keep the current rule — it measures both ad types against one floor, which is the version the player actually experiences. Not worth the change.

05 Referral, squads and the numbers

"10★ on signup, 50★ at level 10, 300★ at first redeem — does that put us in loss, given first redeem is already break-even?"

No. It's a 3× return, and you're reading the wrong ledger.

The first-redeem pack being break-even is a fact about that transaction. The referral bonus is paid against the referred user's whole revenue history, and by the time they redeem they have already generated it:

LineStarsCost
Referrer, at referee level 1050★$0.030
Referrer, at referee first redeem300★$0.180
Total acquisition cost350★$0.21
Ad revenue that referee already generated (30 engaged days)—$0.72

You pay 30% of revenue already banked, after it is banked, for a user who retains better than any bought install. Compare buying that install: $0.05–0.15 in cash, upfront, with no guarantee they ever open the app twice.

The one change

Cut the referrer's signup bonus to zero. Signup is free to fake and proves nothing. Give the referee a small grant on signup — it is a genuine activation lever — and pay the referrer only at level 10 and first redeem, both of which are expensive to fake.

"Milestone ladder — does it trigger on signup, level 10 or first redeem? Signup can be farmed."

Never signup, for exactly the reason you gave. Key every milestone on referee first redeem — the event that proves the referee was a real, engaged human.

And pay the milestones in things that cost XP and caps rather than stars, which is your own VIP reasoning applied one level out:

Completed referralsUnlocksWhat it costs you
3+1 permanent daily game attempt and +1 quiz attemptNothing. Revenue-positive — more attempts means more ads.
5Vanity referral codeNothing.
10Permanent leaderboard bracket upgrade, or a standing 10% discount on every packSmall, and only on redemption.
25Creator tier: dashboard, custom code, direct line to youYour time — and these are the people worth it.

The trick is in row one: paying your best referrers in raised caps makes them watch more ads. The reward and the revenue point the same way.

"Squad redemption — I don't understand it. Explain."

Three to five players form a squad. Each contributes stars into a shared pot. When the pot reaches a pack price, one member receives the pack; the pot resets and the next member is in line.

Why it is the strongest viral mechanic available to you

  • The grind divides. A 780★ pack takes one person 30 days. A four-person squad fills it in about eight. The first payout inside that squad happens in a week — and three people watch it happen.
  • Social obligation replaces motivation. You don't skip a day when three friends are waiting on the pot. This is a far stronger retention force than any streak.
  • The inviter gets a real reason to recruit. Not "get 100 stars" — "I need a fourth". Completely different ask.
  • It mirrors the product. PUBG is a squad game. The mechanic is already the user's mental model.
  • Every payout gets three witnesses instead of one, so proof spreads with no share button at all.

Fraud, and the obvious attack

One person makes four accounts and pools into themselves. Mitigations: every member needs ≥10 active days and a passing integrity check; the pot only pays a member who personally contributed ≥20% of it; members must have distinct device attestations; one squad payout per member per 30 days.

Honest cost

A squads collection, membership management, a contribution ledger, payout election and the UI for all of it. Two to three weeks solo. This is the mechanic that takes you from 5,000 users to 50,000 — not the one that takes you from 0 to 1,000. Build it after first payouts are proven, not before.

"K-factor — what is this?"

How many new users each existing user brings you.

K = (invites sent per user) × (install rate per invite)

Each user sends 3 invites, 20% install → K = 0.6. The compounding multiplier on every other channel is 1 / (1 − K), so at K = 0.6 every 100 users you acquire eventually become 250.

  • K > 1 — self-sustaining exponential growth. Very rare, and not a plan.
  • K ≈ 0.4 — excellent. Every channel is worth 1.67× face value.
  • K < 0.15 — referral is a feature, not a channel.

Measuring it: referral codes successfully redeemed in a period, divided by new users in that period. You can compute it today from referredBy — no new instrumentation needed.

"Midasbuy receipts through Firebase — worth the time per redeem?"

Yes, with three conditions. It is the most credible trust object you can produce, and no reward app does it.

  1. Redact first. The receipt shows your buyer account. Crop to transaction line, player ID, amount, date — or you are publishing your own purchase account to every user.
  2. Store it against the redemption and show it in Orders. Don't push it at them; let them find it and screenshot it. That's the artefact, and it's better shared by them than by you.
  3. Time-box it. ~15 seconds per redeem, so 12 minutes a day at 50/day. Worth it now. Automate or drop above ~100/day.

08 The order

Six phases. Each one is worthless before the one above it, and the channel does not fire until phase four.

PhaseWhatGate to move on
1~2 wksReal ad IDs + mediation. Analytics + star-velocity rollup. Offerwall and the S2S endpoint.An offerwall conversion credits a star, end to end, on your own account. You can read a retention chart.
2~1 wkGuest sign-in. Currency strip and first-earn dialog. Payout ticker. Static timelines. Level table tuned down.A stranger can install, play and understand what stars are without asking you.
3~1 wkPlay Integrity. Time gates. Player-ID hold. Cluster query. Receipt capture.You could survive 200 accounts trying to farm the first pack and would notice within a day.
4~3 wks100 hand-recruited users. Facebook and Discord gaming groups, not your channel. Pay 20 of them in public. Fix what breaks.20 completed real payouts and D7 above 10%. This is the gate that protects the channel — do not skip it.

6ongoingBracketed leaderboard. Paid tournaments. Micro-creators. Squad redemption. Fulfilment help.Profit per DAU crosses zero.
The one sequencing rule that matters more than the rest

Phase 4 comes before phase 5, always. Your channel is a one-shot asset — 150,000 people who trust you, built over years, and it does not regenerate. A hundred hand-held users and twenty completed payouts is a three-week price for finding out whether the app pays reliably. Firing the channel first and discovering the answer in public costs you the channel, the app, and the reputation you would need for the next attempt.

Everything else in these two documents is a preference. This one is not.
